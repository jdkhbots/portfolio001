Personal Design Project, 2026

Parametric
5-Inch Quadcopter Frame

A 3D-printable drone frame designed in Onshape, then refined from a working first layout (v1) into a lighter, load-shaped v2.

Tool Onshape (parametric CAD)
Size 225 mm motor-to-motor, 5-inch props
Material PETG, FDM printing
Result v2 is 23% lighter than v1
View the 3D model in Onshape ↗

The v2 frame with its top plate on four aluminium standoffs

The Brief

A frame built to real drone standards

The goal was a frame a drone manufacturer would recognise: standard part interfaces, safe propeller spacing, and geometry that prints cleanly. Every key dimension is a named variable, so changing the motor-to-motor size or arm section updates the whole model.

01

Standard mounting

16 × 16 mm motor bolt pattern and a 30.5 × 30.5 mm flight-controller stack, the common sizes for 5-inch builds.

02

Propeller clearance

Motors on a 159.1 mm square give 32 mm between neighbouring 127 mm (5-inch) propellers.

03

Printable without supports

Flat-based geometry with no steep overhangs, designed for FDM printing in PETG.

04

Strength where it matters

Material concentrated at the arm roots, where the bending load from each motor is highest.

Design Iterations

From a working layout to a load-shaped frame

v1 proved the layout. v2 removed material where the loads are low and added it where they are high.

v1
Baseline frame
Parametric layout · Feature pattern · Stack and top plate
Variables→ Layout sketch→ Plate + arm→ Circular pattern ×4

A 60 × 60 × 4 mm bottom plate with 12 × 10 mm arms and Ø32 mm motor pads. The pads were sized so each M3 hole keeps about 3 mm of material around it. One arm was modelled and patterned four times, so every arm stays identical. A 3 mm top plate sits on four 25 mm aluminium standoffs, with slots for a battery strap.

Fig. 1 — v1: straight 10 mm arms on a square plate

v2
Tapered arms
10 mm deep at the root, 6 mm at the motor
Arm plane→ Wedge sketch→ Extrude cut→ Pattern ×4

Bending load on an arm is highest where it meets the body and close to zero at the motor. Each arm now tapers from 10 mm deep at the root to 6 mm at the motor pad, cut with a wedge sketched on a plane along the arm. The root keeps its full depth, where the load is highest.

Fig. 2 — Side view: the arm slopes from the body (left) to the motor pad (right)

Fig. 3 — Arm detail: taper, curved root web and motor pad

v2
Blended body
Arm-root fillets increased from 5 mm to 70 mm

Enlarging the fillet where each arm meets the plate turns the square plate and four separate arms into one continuous shape. The curved webs spread stress at the most loaded joint and give the frame its organic outline, with a single parametric feature instead of extra sketches.

Fig. 4 — v2 frame without the top plate: tapered arms and curved body

v2
Hidden wire routing
4 × 2.5 mm channel under each arm

A groove under each arm carries the motor wires from the motor pad to the centre of the frame, keeping them protected and out of the propeller path. Because the groove is open on the underside, it sits against the print bed and still prints without supports.

Fig. 5 — Underside: a wire channel along each arm

Results

v1 compared with v2

Mass and volume from Onshape mass properties, with PETG assigned to the frame. Values are CAD estimates for the printed frame, excluding the top plate and standoffs.
