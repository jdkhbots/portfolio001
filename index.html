Design Improvement of a
Kenkey Dough Mixer
with Manual Override

Redesigning a kenkey production machine to sustain operation during power outages — a common challenge in Ghana — through a dual-mode mechanical drive system.

Discipline Mechanical Engineering
Focus Machine Design & Power Transmission
Output 3 Conceptual Designs + Evaluation
Context Ghana, Local Production

The Problem

A machine that stops when the power does

Kenkey is a fermented corn dumpling central to Ghanaian cuisine. Its production requires mixing high-viscosity corn dough and partially cooking it — a stage called aflata — inside a heated jacket. When power fails mid-process, the existing motor-only machine halts completely, leaving hot dough unmixed and risking both product loss and operator safety.

01

No manual fallback

Power outages halt production entirely with no way to continue mixing manually.

02

Transmission losses

Inefficiencies in the existing drivetrain reduce torque delivered to the stirrer.

03

Maintenance gaps

Complex components are difficult to source and repair in the local Ghanaian market.

04

Safety exposure

Exposed rotating parts and poor ergonomics create risk for operators.

The Solution

A dual-mode drive — motor or handwheel, same shaft

All three redesigned concepts share a core principle: a mechanical coupling that can be engaged or disengaged to switch instantly between electric motor drive and manual handwheel operation. The coupling is the single switching point. When power fails, the operator disconnects it in seconds and takes over at the handwheel. When power returns, the coupling re-engages and the motor resumes.

Three transmission configurations were developed, each routing motor torque and handwheel input differently — using bevel gears, spur gearboxes, and chain-and-sprocket mechanisms — to explore trade-offs in complexity, cost, and maintainability.

Three Concepts

Different transmissions, same principle

Each concept uses a different drivetrain architecture to link the motor and the handwheel to the stirrer shaft.

01
Bevel Gear with Single Spur Gearbox
Horizontal motor · Single gearbox · Shared handwheel rod
Motor→ Coupling→ Bevel Gear→ Spur Gearbox→ Stirrer

The motor mounts horizontally and shares the same horizontal rod as the manual handwheel — placed on the opposite end. A bevel gear converts drive 90° to the vertical rod feeding the gearbox. Disengaging the coupling isolates the motor; the operator turns the handwheel along the same path. Fewest parts of the three concepts.

Fig. 1 — Concept 1 isometric view

02
Dual Spur Gearbox with Bevel Drive
Horizontal motor · Two gearboxes · Motor & handwheel same side
Stirrer→ Gearbox 1→ Bevel Gear→ Gearbox 2⇄ Motor / Handwheel

Two exposed spur gearboxes in series. Gearbox 2 is the split point: one gear connects to the motor via coupling, the other to the handwheel. Both are on the same side of the machine. Offers the highest torque delivery but has the most components and highest fabrication cost.

Fig. 2 — Concept 2 isometric view

03
Bevel Gear with Chain & Sprocket Drive
Vertical motor · Roller chain · Bevel gear handwheel path
Motor→ Chain→ Sprocket→ Spur Gearbox→ Stirrer

Replaces the second gearbox with a roller chain and sprocket. The vertical rod splits: one path goes up through the chain to the vertically mounted motor; the other turns 90° through a bevel gear to the handwheel. Chains and sprockets are cheap and widely available in Ghana — lowest repair cost.

Fig. 3 — Concept 3 isometric view

Mode of Operation

Switching when the power goes out

In Ghana, power outages can occur at any point during production. The coupling between the motor and the drivetrain can be disengaged in seconds, isolating the motor completely and handing control to the operator via the handwheel. No tools required. The stirrer keeps turning.

Motor Mode — Coupling Engaged

Coupling engaged. Motor drives through the transmission. Handwheel rotates passively. Normal operation.

Manual Override — Coupling Disengaged

Coupling removed. Motor isolated. Operator turns handwheel to continue mixing through the same drivetrain path.
