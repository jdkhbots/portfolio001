Manta
Folding Field-Mapping Drone

A 5-inch mapping drone whose arms fold back along its body, so an agriculture officer can carry it in a backpack on a motorbike, and so its arms fold instead of snapping in a crash.

Tool Onshape (Part Studio + Assembly)
Flying size 159.1 mm motor square, 5-inch props
Folded size ≈ 245 × 111 × 88 mm with battery (CAD)
Mechanism All-back fold, magnet crash latch, tool-free pins

Build sequence: body, arms, pins, base plate and skids, then the fold. Animated from the CAD dimensions.

The Brief

Folding with a reason

Folding was not added for looks. The drone is designed around one user: an agriculture extension officer who rides a motorbike between farms and maps fields from the air. Each requirement comes from that trip.

01

Fits a backpack

Folded, nothing sticks out, so it fits a 30 × 20 × 10 cm bag pocket on a bumpy ride.

02

Survives a crash

Every arm folds backward, the same direction a frontal impact pushes it, so a crash swings an arm back instead of breaking it.

03

Repairs in the field

Each arm is a separate part on a pin, so a damaged arm can be replaced on site.

04

Standard parts

16 × 16 mm motor mounts, a 30.5 mm flight-controller stack and 5-inch props, the same interfaces as common FPV parts.

Design

From layout to a working fold

Built in the order a designer would check it: layout, body, hinges, a clearance study, then arms and the assembly.

01
Layout and body
Variables · Arrow outline · Chamfer · Shell
Variables→ Layout sketch→ Extrude 30 mm→ Chamfer + shell

Motor positions are driven by two variables, a 159.1 mm motor square and 127 mm props. The body is an arrow outline extruded 30 mm, with 7 mm chamfers on its top edges for a faceted, stealth-style look and a 2 mm shell to keep it light. The hinge points were placed so the nose keeps about 7 mm of clearance to the front props.

Fig. 1 — Top view, flying

02
Fold-clearance study
Checked every folded state for overlap before building the arms

The first plan copied a common folding-drone layout: front arms fold back, rear arms fold forward. A clearance study showed the rear motors hitting the front hinges and body. Changing to an all-back fold, with the arms on two levels and the fold stop 5° outward, gave zero overlap. It also means every arm breaks away in the direction a frontal crash pushes it.

Fold layoutResult
Front back, rear forwardRear motors hit hinges and body
All back, arms parallel to bodyFront arms clip the rear tabs
All back, 3° outwardNear miss (≈1 mm³ overlap)
All back, 5° outwardZero overlap
03
Hinges and arms
8 mm arms in 8.4 mm clevis gaps · Ø3.2 mm pins

Each hinge is a clevis: two 3.5 mm lugs on the body hold an 8 mm arm with 0.2 mm clearance each side. The front arms sit 13 mm higher than the rear ones, so when folded they pass over the rear hinges. Each arm is a 12 × 8 mm section. Under 10 N of thrust that gives a bending stress of about 6 MPa, a safety factor of about 5 in PETG.

Fig. 2 — Folded, close up: the front arms ride on the upper level, over the rear hinges

04
Assembly and fold
Revolute mates · Limits · Named positions
Fix body→ Revolute ×4→ Limits→ Flying / Folded

Each arm turns on a revolute mate, limited between its flying angle and its folded stop: 137° for the front arms and 45.2° for the rear. Two named positions switch the whole drone between flying and folded.

Fig. 3 — Flying

Fig. 4 — Folded, from the same angle

05
Crash latch: hard stop + magnets
Sized from the prop's reaction torque

Each spinning prop twists its arm about the hinge pin, up to about 0.1 N·m at full throttle. A spring-ball detent near the pin would need a spring of about 40 N, which won't fit in an 8 mm arm. Instead each arm rests against a stop fin on the body and is held there by a pair of Ø6 × 3 mm magnets 20 mm from the pin, which hold about 0.2 N·m. That is twice the full-throttle torque, yet a knock of about 3 N at the motor pulls the arm free so it folds back instead of snapping. Push it back and it clicks home.

Fig. 5 — Stop fin and magnet pocket, arm part-folded

06
Field repair and underside
Quick-release pins · Removable plate · Replaceable skids

Each hinge uses a standard Ø3 × 20 mm clevis pin held by a hairpin clip, so a damaged arm swaps without tools. The rear pin heads sit in 1.2 mm counterbores because the folded front arms pass just above them. A 3 mm base plate closes the body, carries a standard 30.5 mm flight-controller stack and a downward camera window, and screws into heat-set inserts. The same four screws hold two printed landing skids, which lift the drone 20 mm on a stance about 80 mm wide. A broken skid is a reprint and four screws.

Fig. 6 — Underside: base plate, camera window, pins and skids
